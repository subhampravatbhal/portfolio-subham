How to Sell Your Indian Property as an NRI: Step-by-Step Guide

For Non-Resident Indians (NRIs), liquidating real estate holdings in India represents an incredible wealth transition step. However, navigating remote transactional legalities, complex Indian tax codes (TDS compliance), and FEMA regulations can feel completely overwhelming. This comprehensive guide outlines the step-by-step procedure required to execute a smooth sale.

1. The Legal Prerequisites

Before putting your property on the market, you must establish perfect legal authority. Essential documents include:

  • Original Title Deeds: Clear ownership documents and municipal registrations.
  • Encumbrance Certificate (EC): Demonstrating that the property is completely free from outstanding bank loans or legal disputes.
  • Power of Attorney (POA): If you cannot travel to India to execute the transaction, registering a specific Power of Attorney in favor of a trusted family member is required.
"Tax compliance is the single most critical aspect of NRI property sales. Neglecting TDS withholding rates under Section 195 can lock your sales revenue in escrow audits for years."

2. Demystifying TDS and Section 195

When a resident buys property from an NRI, they are legally required to withhold Tax Deducted at Source (TDS) under Section 195.

Standard TDS rates stand at a staggering 20.6% on the *total sales value* (for long-term capital gains, held over 2 years). This is extremely high since your actual profit might be far lower than 20% of the sale value!

The Solution: You must proactively apply for a **Lower TDS Certificate** from the Income Tax department in India before closing the transaction. This instructs the buyer to deduct taxes *only on your actual capital gains* rather than the total sales amount, saving significant initial cash flow.

3. Repatriating the Funds

Under FEMA regulations, sale proceeds must be deposited directly into your **NRO account**. Once the capital gains tax is paid and standard Form 15CA/15CB tax clearances are certified by a chartered accountant, you can seamlessly repatriate up to **USD 1 Million per financial year** to your foreign bank account.
